Get hands-on help

Help redesigning an assignment, writing an AI policy, building course materials, or experimenting with AI tools.

Request a consult with CATLR →Northeastern's teaching center matches you with a consultant for one assignment or a whole course. Online or in person, no preparation needed.

AI Instructional AssistantsCATLR-managed graduate assistants who support defined projects: building course materials or AI tools, running student surveys and focus groups, and scanning practices in your field. IA projects are arranged through the CSSH directors, so email us with your idea. It doesn't need to be fully formed. AI Makerspace, with MicrosoftWalk-in AI help, faculty consultations, hands-on workshops, and access to AI tools. No AI experience required. In Snell Library (Boston), opening September 24, 2026. They can also host or promote a workshop you run, find an expert for a class session, and offer Microsoft and Northeastern AI badges and credentials students can earn. AI Innovation LabStart with a problem, leave with a working system: student consultant teams, guided by faculty, build custom AI systems over 6 weeks to 6+ months. Teach a project-based course? The Lab can also match a real client AI project to your course objectives. Email ai-innovation@northeastern.edu.

Ready-made AI materials for your course

Modules, guides, and course materials from CSSH and across Northeastern that you can bring into a class or share with students.

DITI & AI (CSSH NULab)The Digital Integration Teaching Initiative's statement on AI, teaching materials for specific AI applications, and a critical AI literacy approach built for humanities and social science courses. New materials are posted here as they're released. Partner with DITIDITI works with CSSH instructors to bring digital and AI modules into a course, with instructional support. In development: a module on AI for literature reviews, and student and educator guides to AI use and pedagogy. DITI Faculty Guide to AI ResourcesA one-page map of Northeastern AI resources (library tutorials, ITS training, CATLR policies) for deciding whether and how to bring AI into a course. AI in Education bibliographyDITI's public Zotero library of sources on AI and education, for course readings or your own background. CS 1700 · Society of Minds: AI for EveryoneKhoury's AI literacy course for students who aren't studying computer science. Recommend it to your students, or borrow from its public course site: session-by-session schedule and a plain-language AI glossary. AI at CSSHThe college's page on AI programs, research, and faculty, useful for pointing students to AI-related courses and certificates.
