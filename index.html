AI in Teaching

Practical, discipline-neutral resources to help CSSH faculty teach with and about AI — keep what makes your teaching yours, and help students become genuinely AI-ready.

AI didn't create a cheating crisis — it exposed how we assess. When a machine can complete a take-home task without learning anything, the task was measuring the product, not the thinking. That's a design problem we know how to solve.

And "AI-ready" isn't "can use ChatGPT." It's judgment — knowing when to use these tools, evaluating their output, using them ethically, and adding the human value on top. Not every course must use AI: the goal is that every program gives students some exposure. Ring-fencing AI-free courses is entirely fine.

6 Sources & credits

Grounding documents (Northeastern)

  • AI Readiness Framework — Northeastern Curricular Transformation Initiative. Defines AI literacy and fluency across four dimensions (Data, Critical Thinking, Ethics, Human-Centered Creativity).
  • Assessment in an AI-Enabled World: A Faculty Guide — Northeastern's Task Force on AI and Assessment. Source of the "not a cheating crisis" reframe and several assessment-redesign principles.
  • Weaving AI Readiness into the Northeastern Curriculum — the AI Curriculum Working Group. Quick-start framing for AI in the curriculum.
  • Standards & Recommendations for the Use of Generative AI in Teaching and Learning — Northeastern. Referenced for example AI-use policy language.

CSSH-created materials

  • Discussion Prompt Redesign Checklist (the "four levers") — Linda Kowalcky, CSSH Director of AI Curricular Transformation.
  • Interactive teaching demos, the AI Lab & the replication interview — Richeng Piao, CSSH Director of AI Teaching Innovation (from his ECON courses).
