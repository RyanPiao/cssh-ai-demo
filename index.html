AI in Teaching

Practical help for teaching with and about AI — whether you want to keep it out of your course, try it once, or build it in.

You decide how AI fits your course. Northeastern's standards leave that choice to each instructor: no course is required to use AI, and prohibiting it is allowed. This site helps you make the choice, follow the university's rules, and design assignments that match.

Three rules to know before you start

  • Don't rely on AI detectors. They're unreliable, and using one needs prior approval from the university's AI Review Committee.
  • Using AI to grade essays or other open-ended work needs AI Review Committee review first.
  • Keep student and other personal information out of AI tools unless the use has been reviewed. Point students to university-supported tools such as the NU Claude Portal.

Why rethink assessment now?

When an AI tool can complete a take-home task without the student learning anything, the task may be measuring the finished product rather than the thinking behind it. Northeastern's faculty guide Assessment in an AI-Enabled World frames this as a design problem, and design problems are ones we can work on together.

Being “AI-ready” also means more than knowing how to use a chatbot. It means judgment: knowing when a tool helps, checking what it produces, using it ethically, and adding human value on top.
